          A Public Guide to Vaelora and the Cart City of Gantry

          Kept on the counter at the Carters' Hall for anyone waiting on a rank. It tells you what any carter in Gantry knows, and it is fairly accurate. The Hall reminds readers that a carter's "fairly" covers a lot of road.

          Skim sections 1 to 4. Read 5 to 8: that is the town you are in.

          1. The world in one page

          Vaelora is a world where magic is infrastructure, not spectacle. A magical energy called resonance flows through underground channels called ley lines, pools where several cross (nexus points), and thins where they do not. Most people live near nexus points because crops grow, trade runs, and magic is easy there. Magic is real, regulated and a little tiring. It is a trade, with licences, and sometimes it fails.

          It is Year 392 of the Third Age.

          AgeWhat everyone is taught
          The First AgeGiants built the Crystal Spires and the great First Library, and mapped the ley lines. Then the Rupture, a catastrophic magical event about three thousand years ago, ended their civilisation and tore a permanent wound in the world: the Rift.
          The Second AgeChaos. False Kings claimed authority from gods they had made rather than worshipped. A bard called the Truth-Singer exposed the fraud. It ended with new laws: no ruler may claim divine right.
          The Third Age (now)Recovery. Institutions that write everything down. A Rift in the far south that everyone is managing and no one has solved.

          The Rift. In the deep south (South Tyriania), reality is damaged: spatial distortion, failing magic, creatures changed in ways that do not follow natural law. It has been monitored for about eighty years. It grows slowly. Everyone says it is stable; most of the people saying so are being careful with the word.

          Three long-running troubles your character has heard about and mostly ignores:

          1. The First Library, a rediscovered Giant archive, is fought over by the great institutions.
          2. The Hollow Veins, dead ley lines under the continents, are said to be changing.
          3. Old institutional bargains are straining under a century of magical progress.

          Tone. Investigative. Morally complicated. Magic-rich. Not grimdark: bad things happen, but there is hope and warmth. Institutions do bad things for reasons you can follow.

          2. How magic works

          • Everyone carries Resonitae, a biological substrate that answers to language and intent. People with strong resonance are Resonant-Marked. People with none are Magic-Mute. Neither is better. Magic-Mute people often perceive what casters miss, and many are respected seers, craftspeople and bards.
          • You cast by speaking Vaelan, the common language, in a precise register. Every incantation is spoken twice: the first pass shapes intent, the second releases it. Interrupt the second pass and it misfires.
          ForceIn a sentence
          Arcaen (Building)Trained, structured, licensed; the institutions' kind
          Vitalen (Growing)Inherited and accumulated over generations; old blood, old families, mountain and ice communities
          Somalen (Dreaming)Collective belief and ritual; priests, wind-readers, shrine communities
          Aelum (Being)Ancient and rare; very few living people carry it. Not available to new characters
          • Dialects of Vaelan: Eldorian (the standard, used by institutions), Verdant (Valo elves), Frostholm (the frozen north), Emberisle (forge and volcano communities), Crystalian (the ice islands). Mixing dialects in an incantation is allowed and inadvisable.
          • The First Tongue is older and different: where Vaelan requests, it decrees. Fragments exist. They are classified; if you find one, the law says to report it to a licensed institution.
          • Where magic runs thin or thick: thick near nexus points and big cities; disrupted near the Rift (more misfires); nothing at all in the dead Hollow Veins.
          • What magic can do: enhance bodies, make barriers, send messages, read resonance, heal slowly, enchant objects. What it cannot easily do: raise the dead, convincingly lie to a truth-sensitive listener, or open a Rift (illegal, catastrophic, absolutely prohibited).

          Your licence matters. A caster certified Journeyman or above is a licensed professional. Casting uncertified in public is illegal in some places. Gantry rarely asks for it, but people notice.

          3. Who runs things

          FactionWhat everyone knowsWhat people quietly suspect

          The Arcane UniversityThe innovative rival; recruits widely; "creative, accessible, occasionally reckless"Its director is building something

          Money: Gold Crown (rare; institutions), Silver Mark (SM; everyday), Copper Bit (small change). 1 Crown = 100 Marks = 1,000 Bits. A decent meal and a bed in an average city costs 2 to 3 SM. A good rune-inscribed item starts near 150 SM. Resonance Chips pay for magical services.

          Law is regional, but the same few rules appear almost everywhere:

          1. No divine authority: a ruler's power must be justified by what they do.
          2. Bardic witness is primary evidence. A bard who announces they are witnessing makes everything said afterward legally weighty. People ask bards to leave before delicate talks.
          3. Sealed arts are prohibited, and the institutions enforce it.
          4. Open roads: the Flaneur Charter guarantees merchants freedom of passage and enforces contracts.

          Standing is earned three ways: magical ability, institutional affiliation, economic power. They do not always agree. A Magic-Mute master craftsperson outranks a Resonant-Marked apprentice in practical terms.

          Customs that carry weight: a meal-sealed bargain (struck over a shared meal before any witness) is binding. Do not name the dead aloud for three days. Do not enter a shrine whose god has faded. Do not speak incantations in anger. The Day of Veiling (the Rupture's anniversary) is for lanterns and no spells.

          Travel: between big cities, expect 4 to 16 days, depending on route (sea is faster). Roads are better near live ley lines, worse near dead ones.

          4. Golgotha: the country that is a business

          A mountain continent of mining guilds with no central government. Whichever guild holds the mineral rights holds the ground. Forge-cities stand at shaft-heads. Law is guild law, plus the Flaneur Charter where a guild has signed it. Its north is ice, its middle lava, its south arable. Eleven forge-cities share it, each run by the guild that built it.

          5. Gantry, the cart city

          The Transport Guild's city, about 30,000 people. Gantry makes nothing. It sells movement: a cart's place in the queue, loading, signal traffic, and the certificates that prove all three. Ore from the eastern seams, pig iron, grain, timber, lamp oil and rope all come through it.

          QuarterWhat it is
          The YardsThe marshalling ground: forty-six gantries in six rows, weigh-sheds, chain stores, lamp-masts. Loud, dusty, never empty
          Carters' RowThe cart-masters' stone houses, a painted wheel on every lintel. Old money
          The Rank BenchA small square of clerks, notaries and one court. Everything here is paper
          Tallow StreetThe warehouse street on the southern edge, where the eastern line comes in. Cheap, loud, and watched less closely than the Row
          Spoke TownHousing along the five cart-lines out of town: draymen, farriers, wheelwrights, rope-makers

          Landmarks. The Queue Roll, a wall of numbered chalk-and-brass boards, one per licensed cart. The Signal Loft on the Carters' Hall roof, first drum-and-lamp post of the eastern line. The Stand Stone, where the carters sat for eleven weeks in Year 206 and moved nothing. The Scale-Shed Bell, which rings tomorrow's ranks at noon.

          Weather. Dry and gritty. Wind off the slag plain lifts dust, and you taste it in the bread. Washing hung out in Gantry dries grey.

          6. Getting by

          • The Marshal's Gate. A yard-marshal in a brown coat takes your cart, asks the load and the destination, and hangs a tin rank chit on your shaft. People look at your chit, then at you, and decide what you are.
          • Yard-marshals fine on the spot: for jumping a rank, a missing chit, a cart left in a lane.
          • Strangers. A visitor with a carrier's licence or a cart-master's letter gets a hearing at the Bench. A visitor with neither is fined, not heard, and may spend a night in the shed behind the Scale-Shed Bell. A day-pass from the Marshal's Gate (4 SM) turns you into a visitor with paper.

          A day in town. The Yards start at dawn as the lamp-masts turn green. Chit lines are longest at mid-morning. The noon bell reads out tomorrow's ranks, and petitions go to the Bench in the afternoon. At dusk the yards clear and the Night Lane opens: a strip along Tallow Street kept for waste, dung and anything the Hall has decided needs no rank.

          7. Tallow Street and the Lamp and Pick

          Tallow Street runs east to west along the southern edge of the Yards. Its warehouses are tallow-brick, long, low and windowless, with loading doors on the street and a hoist-beam over each. By day it is loud with carters. After the last bell it goes quiet, except for the doors.

          The Lamp and Pick stands at the corner of Tallow Street and the first spoke: the last inn before the yards and the first after the plain. Its landlord, Hob Pettigrew, is a one-armed man of fifty who lost the arm to a chain-hoist and talks about it only if you ask twice. He serves carters, hostlers, surveyors' runners and anyone who pays. He is discreet to a fault. Breakfast is in the common room. The noticeboard by the door carries rank-chit offers, lost dogs and stranger things.

          "The best thing about an inn is that nothing said in it ever happened." Hob Pettigrew

          Gantry speech is short and flat: carters shout across a yard and then wait.

          9. The Survey Dispatch Board

          A clearing office in Golgotha's charter south that contracts licensed freelancers to look at things and attest: to state, signed, what they observed and not what they were told. A Board licence is a stamped card. Holders turn up in odd places, and clerks tend to treat them as a person with paper.

          This guide is checked against the Hall's own notices. The Hall has not checked its notices against the street.

          Modifier = floor((score - 10) / 2), rounded down. Your class sets a minimum for its key scores: if your roll is lower, the DM raises it to the class number. HP, AC and your resource pools are finished by the DM.

          The three pools

          • HP: as 5e.
          • RP (Resonance Points): your casting resource. One pool, from Intelligence and class.
          • SP (Stamina Points): for physical exertion; used by martial classes.

          Casting

          An incantation has up to five parts, said twice:

          Invocation, Direction, Target, (Modifier), Binding

          • The first recitation shapes the intent; the second releases it. Hold between them to amplify. Interrupted? It misfires (the DM rolls a d10: +2 near the Rift, -1 at a nexus).
          • You do not need to speak real Vaelan. Describe what you say. A badly worded incantation does exactly what was said.

          Money, level and rest

          At the table

          • Lines and Veils: tell the DM anything you do not want in play. An X-card or a thumbs-down pauses the scene, no questions.
          • Players roll their own dice. The DM shows every enemy roll.
          • You may ask the DM "what would my character know about this?" any time.

          How we play

          The DM writes every turn here. You read it, decide, and answer in the app.

          The loop

          1. Install the app. On iPhone, open this page in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. On Android, use the browser menu, then Install app. It opens from your home screen and stays live while it is open.
          2. Open your inbox. Go to Inbox, choose your character and type your inbox code. Your profile, sheet and the DM's letters are there.
          3. Read the turn. Each turn appears in Table within seconds of the DM posting it.
          4. Answer in Your move. Everyone at the table sees it. The first answer moves the story.
          5. Roll your own dice. When the DM calls a roll, type the die result in its box. No dice: use the Dice tab, or press Roll for me. The DM adds your bonuses.
          6. Secrets go in Private chat. Only you and the DM see it. A private roll shows up there too.

          Inboxes are sealed: each opens only with its own code. The host plays too, so the host never reads your sheet or your letters.

          Where it starts

          Gantry, Tallow Street, the Lamp and Pick, early morning. Your character has a room here. Breakfast is on in the common room.

          The tone

          A story-first game about people with fears, loyalties and bad days. Expect mysteries with real answers, institutions that do bad things for reasons you can follow, and paperwork that matters. Do not expect a chosen-hero power fantasy.

          Failure is part of the story. A failed roll means something happened that you did not intend, never that nothing happened.

          • Be specific about intent. Not "I search" but "I check the cart's chit against the Roll."
          • Ask "what would my character know about this?" at any time.
          • Your character acts on what they know, not on what you guess.

          Safety

          Lines never appear in the game. Veils happen off-screen. Send yours to the DM by file at any time; you do not need to explain.

          Pause word: X. Anyone can say it, at any time, and the scene stops. No questions asked.

          How the DM runs it

          • DCs: 10 easy, 15 medium, 20 hard. Unsure: 12, with advantage for a clever approach.
          • The DM shows every roll it makes for an enemy.
          • Decisions are yours. The DM presents situations; you choose.
