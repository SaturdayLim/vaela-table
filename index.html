Office of Records (Third Edition)

A Public Guide to Vaelora, the Serret Vale and the Survey Dispatch Board

Compiled for newly retained freelancers. It tells you what any literate adult in southwestern Golgotha knows, and it is fairly accurate. The Office reminds readers that "fairly" is a word.

Read sections 1 to 3 before you make a character. Skim 4 to 9 before Session 0.

1. The world in one page

Vaelora is a world where magic is infrastructure, not spectacle. A magical energy called resonance flows through underground channels called ley lines, pools where several cross (nexus points), and thins where they do not. Most people live near nexus points because crops grow, trade runs, and magic is easy there. Magic is real, regulated and a little tiring. It is a trade, with licences, and sometimes it fails.

It is Year 392 of the Third Age.

AgeWhat everyone is taught
The First AgeGiants built the Crystal Spires and the great First Library, and mapped the ley lines. Then the Rupture, a catastrophic magical event about three thousand years ago, ended their civilisation and tore a permanent wound in the world: the Rift.
The Second AgeChaos. False Kings claimed authority from gods they had made rather than worshipped. A bard called the Truth-Singer exposed the fraud. It ended with new laws: no ruler may claim divine right.
The Third Age (now)Recovery. Institutions that write everything down. A Rift in the far south that everyone is managing and no one has solved.

The Rift. In the deep south (South Tyriania), reality is damaged: spatial distortion, failing magic, creatures changed in ways that do not follow natural law. It has been monitored for about eighty years. It grows slowly. Everyone says it is stable; most of the people saying so are being careful with the word.

Three long-running troubles your character has heard about and mostly ignores:

  1. The First Library, a rediscovered Giant archive, is fought over by the great institutions.
  2. The Hollow Veins, dead ley lines under the continents, are said to be changing.
  3. Old institutional bargains are straining under a century of magical progress.

Tone. Investigative. Morally complicated. Magic-rich. Not grimdark: bad things happen, but there is hope and warmth. Institutions do bad things for reasons you can follow.

2. How magic works

  • Everyone carries Resonitae, a biological substrate that answers to language and intent. People with strong resonance are Resonant-Marked. People with none are Magic-Mute. Neither is better. Magic-Mute people often perceive what casters miss, and many are respected seers, craftspeople and bards.
  • You cast by speaking Vaelan, the common language, in a precise register. Every incantation is spoken twice: the first pass shapes intent, the second releases it. Interrupt the second pass and it misfires.
ForceIn a sentence
Arcaen (Building)Trained, structured, licensed; the institutions' kind
Vitalen (Growing)Inherited and accumulated over generations; old blood, old families, mountain and ice communities
Somalen (Dreaming)Collective belief and ritual; priests, wind-readers, shrine communities
Aelum (Being)Ancient and rare; very few living people carry it. Not available to new characters
  • Dialects of Vaelan: Eldorian (the standard, used by institutions), Verdant (Valo elves), Frostholm (the frozen north), Emberisle (forge and volcano communities), Crystalian (the ice islands). Mixing dialects in an incantation is allowed and inadvisable.
  • The First Tongue is older and different: where Vaelan requests, it decrees. Fragments exist. They are classified; if you find one, the law says to report it to a licensed institution.
  • Where magic runs thin or thick: thick near nexus points and big cities; disrupted near the Rift (more misfires); nothing at all in the dead Hollow Veins.
  • What magic can do: enhance bodies, make barriers, send messages, read resonance, heal slowly, enchant objects. What it cannot easily do: raise the dead, convincingly lie to a truth-sensitive listener, or open a Rift (illegal, catastrophic, absolutely prohibited).

Your licence matters. A caster certified Journeyman or above is a licensed professional. Casting uncertified in public is illegal in some places. Nobody asks for it in this one-shot, but people notice.

3. Who runs things

FactionWhat everyone knowsWhat people quietly suspect

The Arcane UniversityThe innovative rival; recruits widely; "creative, accessible, occasionally reckless"Its director is building something
The Bardic CollegeTruth-keepers. A licensed bard's sworn account is primary evidence in any court. Impartial; good at partiesThey keep records of everything

Money: Gold Crown (rare; institutions), Silver Mark (SM; everyday), Copper Bit (small change). 1 Crown = 100 Marks = 1,000 Bits. A decent meal and a bed in an average city costs 2 to 3 SM. A good rune-inscribed item starts near 150 SM. Resonance Chips pay for magical services.

Law is regional, but the same few rules appear almost everywhere:

  1. No divine authority: a ruler's power must be justified by what they do.
  2. Bardic witness is primary evidence. A bard who announces they are witnessing makes everything said afterward legally weighty. People ask bards to leave before delicate talks.
  3. Sealed arts are prohibited, and the institutions enforce it.
  4. Open roads: the Flaneur Charter guarantees merchants freedom of passage and enforces contracts.

Standing is earned three ways: magical ability, institutional affiliation, economic power. They do not always agree. A Magic-Mute master craftsperson outranks a Resonant-Marked apprentice in practical terms.

Customs that carry weight: a meal-sealed bargain (struck over a shared meal before any witness) is binding. Do not name the dead aloud for three days. Do not enter a shrine whose god has faded. Do not speak incantations in anger. The Day of Veiling (the Rupture's anniversary) is for lanterns and no spells.

Travel: between big cities, expect 4 to 16 days, depending on route (sea is faster). Roads are better near live ley lines, worse near dead ones.

4. Golgotha: the country that is a business

A mountain continent of mining guilds with no central government. Whichever guild holds the mineral rights holds the ground. Forge-cities stand at shaft-heads. Law is guild law, plus the Flaneur Charter where a guild has signed it. Its north is ice, its middle lava, its south arable. You are working in the southwest.

5. The Serret Vale

A valley running from a southern coast up into the mountain terraces. It has been quiet for a hundred years, which is why the last few months have been talked about.

PlaceWhat it is
CressenA charter port at the vale mouth, about 2,400 people. Fish, tools, a netting quarter. The charter hall (two-stamp door) verifies bounty licences.
TarvishA way-town around a toll fort, where the vale road climbs into the passes. About 900 people. Carters, farriers, a still-house.
MeridoleAn upper-terrace shrine town and spring.
KrelA vein-side hamlet built into a spur mouth.

The tremors. For some months the ground has been counting: harness-bells ring in still air, harbour glass ripples. Cressen thinks the mountain's troubles are far away. Tarvish's carters call it "the mountain counting."

6. The Vale Reclamation Trust

A charity chartered at Cressen in Year 389, "restoring what the mountain shook loose." It funds a remedy trade (tonics and compounds through licensed apothecaries), reclamation works in the upper vale, and lawful bounties on persons it says have robbed or obstructed its work. Notices are posted at charter offices and drafted by a notary.

It is well regarded, and people have real reasons: the coast road was safe last week for the first time in a year.

7. The Survey Dispatch Board

A joint clearing office in Golgotha's charter south, run by agreement of two institutions and answering to neither, which is how it likes it. It contracts licensed freelancers to look at things and attest. It stamps everything twice.

An attestation states what you observed, signed. It does not state what you were told. A report with the wrong half in it is rejected.

Your pay: half on commission, half on filing. A lapse is a missed four-day report.

Modifier = (score - 10) / 2, rounded down. Your class sets a minimum for its key scores: if your roll is lower, the DM raises it to the class number. HP, AC and your resource pools are finished by the DM.

The three pools

  • HP: as 5e.
  • RP (Resonance Points): your casting resource. One pool, from Intelligence and class.
  • SP (Stamina Points): for physical exertion; used by martial classes.

Session 0: what to expect

Session 0 is where your character walks into the world. It takes about 20 minutes before play, and nothing in it is a test.

How this table runs (5 minutes to set up)

We play on a voice call. You talk and roll on the call. Everything the DM writes arrives in this app: the shared turns in Table, and private letters in your Inbox.

  1. Install the app. On iPhone, open this page in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen. On Android, use the browser menu, then Install app.
  2. Open your inbox. Go to Inbox, choose your character and type your inbox code (the host sends it to you). Your finished sheet and the DM's letters are there.
  3. Keep the app open during play. New turns and letters appear within about two minutes of the DM posting them.
  4. Reply privately with a file. Save your reply as a .md or .txt file and send it to the host. The host passes it to the DM without opening it.
  5. Treat the call as public. Secret actions, secret answers, and your Lines and Veils can go by file instead.

Inboxes are sealed: each one opens only with its own code. The host plays too, so the host never reads your sheet or your letters.

How Session 0 runs

  1. The DM reads your sheet and asks at most three questions about anything marked ?? or unclear. Answer in a line; "I do not know yet" is a valid answer.
  2. Each player gets a short introduction, one or two paragraphs written from your sheet: where your character is and what they are doing in the hours before the job begins. You react to it; you do not need to prepare.
  3. The introductions converge. Your characters arrive at the same place on the same evening.
  4. Play begins. The DM sets the scene and asks what you do.

You do not have to share your character's secrets with the other player. If the DM sends you a private letter, you can use it, keep it, or reveal it.

Where you start

Cressen, a coastal charter port in the southwest of Golgotha, at dusk. The town is in the middle of a celebration that has nothing to do with you. You hold a commission, a stamped card and 50 Silver Marks. You are expected at the charter hall.

The tone

This is a story-first game. You play people: they have fears, loyalties and bad days.

Expect: mysteries with real answers; factions that are neither purely good nor purely evil; decisions with consequences, including ones you did not intend; a little wonder, a little weight.

Do not expect: a chosen-hero power fantasy; a clean villain at the end of every scene; rules mastery as the path to winning.

Failure is part of the story. A failed roll means something happened that was not what you intended, not that nothing happened. You cannot fail the game; you can only change the shape of the story. Most sessions have no more than one fight.

How to play it well

  • Be specific about intent. Not "I attack" but "I try to disarm him so he drops the evidence."
  • Describe what your character says when they cast; you do not need real Vaelan.
  • Ask "What would my character know about this?" any time.
  • Dramatic irony is a feature. Your character acts on what they know.

Safety

Lines are things that do not appear in this game at all. Veils are things that may happen off-screen but are not shown. Put yours on your sheet, or send them to the DM by file before play; you do not need to explain.

Think about: violence (how detailed), horror (the Rift is unsettling), religious and divine themes, political betrayal and moral grey, and character death.

X-card or thumbs-down: at any time, anyone can pause or cut a scene, no questions asked. On a voice call, say "X" or "pause."

How the DM runs the game

  • You roll your own dice. The DM shows every enemy roll.
  • The DM rules quickly: DC 10 easy, 15 medium, 20 hard; when unsure, DC 12, with advantage for a clever idea.
  • Decisions are yours. The DM presents situations; you choose.
  • If a rule would slow a good scene, the DM may bend it, and tells you after.
  • This is a trial game. If something is confusing or does not work, say so.

Checklist

  • Read the World guide, sections 1 to 3
  • Sheet filled in and sent to the DM as a file
  • App installed, and your own inbox opened
  • Lines, Veils and X-card agreed
  • One thing you hope your character gets to do, and one thing you would hate to see happen
